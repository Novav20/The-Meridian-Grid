### 1. Ubiquitous Language

- **Term**: ALARMS
- **Definition**: A modular multi-agent framework mapping Endsley’s Situational Awareness levels and IDA macrocognition to specialized cognitive agents drawing on Signal Detection Theory and vigilance research.

> **Evidence**: "we propose ALARMS (Analysis, Locutor, Anticipation, Risk Manager, Simulation) – a modular multi-agent framework mapping Endsley’s Situational Awareness (SA) levels and IDA macrocognition to specialised agents, drawing on Signal Detection Theory and vigilance research." (p. 1)

- **Term**: Cognitive Mirror
- **Definition**: An AI agent paradigm designed to explicitly support human metacognition, reflect and challenge operator reasoning, and maintain human oversight to prevent deskilling and automation bias.

> **Evidence**: "Contemporary design doctrines therefore advocate a move away from all-knowing ‘oracle’ systems towards cognitive mirror agents that explicitly support human metacognition, challenge assumptions, and maintain human oversight." (p. 3)

- **Term**: Analysis Agent
- **Definition**: An agent implementing Level 1 and lower-Level 2 situation awareness by transforming heterogeneous sensor and log data into a machine- and human-consumable 5W+H (Who, What, When, Where, Why, How) situation model.

> **Evidence**: "The Analysis agent implements Level 1 and lower-Level 2 situation awareness by transforming heterogeneous sensor and log data into a 5W+H (Who, What, When, Where, Why, How) situation model that is machine- and human-consumable." (p. 5)

- **Term**: Locutor Agent
- **Definition**: The primary human-AI interface operating as a cognitive mirror, combining LLM-based natural language understanding/generation and XAI to generate situation reports, interpret operator queries, and challenge operator assumptions.

> **Evidence**: "The Locutor agent is designed as the primary human – AI interface and as a cognitive mirror rather than a simple dialogue front-end." (p. 5)

- **Term**: Anticipation Agent
- **Definition**: An agent implementing Level 3 situation awareness and weak-signal analysis by learning baselines of normal behavior and detecting explicit anomalies as well as absences of expected events.

> **Evidence**: "The Anticipation agent is designed to realise Level 3 situation awareness and weak-signal analysis by learning baselines of ‘normal’ behaviour and detecting both explicit anomalies and absences of expected events." (p. 5)

- **Term**: Risk Manager Agent
- **Definition**: An agent embodying decision support by mapping events and anomalies onto critical assets, computing probabilistic risk via likelihood and impact, and generating structured mitigation recommendations.

> **Evidence**: "The Risk Manager agent embodies the decision-support component of macrocognitive models such as IDA/IDHEAS." (p. 5)

- **Term**: Simulation Agent
- **Definition**: An agent operationalizing operational testing, what-if analysis, and operator training using discrete-event, agent-based, or digital-twin simulation.

> **Evidence**: "The Simulation agent operationalises ALARMS as both an operational and a training/experimentation environment." (p. 5)

### 2. Domain Rules and Invariants

- **Rule**: Structural Enforceability of Human Override
- **Mechanism**: The human operator maintains exclusive operational authority; at the interaction cycle's decision phase, the operator can query agents via the Locutor, modify recommendations, or execute vetoes, which are logged alongside rationale.

> **Evidence**: "the operator interrogates any agent via the Locutor, accepts, modifies, or vetoes AI recommendations, and logs decisions with rationale – human override is structurally enforced at this stage." (p. 6)

- **Rule**: Mandatory Uncertainty Propagation
- **Mechanism**: Detection and projection outputs generated by the Anticipation agent must explicitly incorporate confidence estimates and alternative hypotheses, which are mandatorily forwarded to the Risk Manager and Locutor agents.

> **Evidence**: "A core design principle is explicit uncertainty management: all outputs include confidence estimates and alternative hypotheses, which are propagated to the Risk Manager and Locutor." (p. 5)

- **Rule**: Metacognitive Restructuring via Cognitive Mirroring
- **Mechanism**: The Locutor agent re-frames situations in alternative perspectives, highlights uncertainties, and directly challenges operator assumptions during dialogue, forcing continuous active sensemaking to counter automation complacency and deskilling.

> **Evidence**: "Interaction patterns are engineered so that the Locutor can restate situations in alternative frames, surface uncertainty, and challenge operator assumptions, thereby actively sustaining macrocognitive processes and mitigating automation bias and complacency." (p. 5)

### 3. Architectural Constraints and Patterns

- **Constraint/Pattern**: Shared Representation Space with Orthogonal Locutor Interrogation Topology
- **Implication**: Primary analytical agents (Analysis, Anticipation, Risk Manager, Simulation) exchange data asynchronously via message-passing, publish-subscribe, or blackboard-style shared state around a 5W+H model, while the Locutor maintains dedicated orthogonal query APIs to every individual agent.

> **Evidence**: "The Analysis agent publishes 5W+H situation descriptions to a shared representational space, which is accessed by the Anticipation, Risk Manager, and Simulation agents. A combination of message-passing, publish – subscribe, and blackboard- style shared state supports decoupling and extensibility while preserving a coherent situation model. The Locutor sits orthogonally to this flow, with dedicated APIs to query each agent." (p. 5)

- **Constraint/Pattern**: Non-Binding Advisory Output Constraint
- **Implication**: The Risk Manager agent is architecturally constrained to output non-binding mitigation recommendations, trade-off evaluations, and residual risks, preventing the automated execution of binding control decisions without operator approval.

> **Evidence**: "It generates structured recommendations – possible mitigation options, their trade-offs, and residual risks – rather than binding decisions, ensuring that the human operator remains the final authority." (p. 5)

### 4. Context Assumptions

- **Assumption**: Tacit and Embodied Expertise Incompressibility

> **Evidence**: "Expert operators rely on multimodal, often inarticulable cues and years of experience to make rapid, intuitive judgements in rare or abnormal situations, which are difficult to capture in purely computational terms." (p. 3)

- **Assumption**: Unrealistic Data Quality and Edge Infrastructure Assumptions in Legacy Deployments

> **Evidence**: "Assumptions about data quality and infrastructure (e.g. edge computing) prove unrealistic in legacy systems." (p. 9)

- **Assumption**: Biological Limits of Human Attentional Bandwidth under Alarm Floods

> **Evidence**: "In domains such as urban/transport surveillance, cybersecurity operations centres, and industrial process control rooms, operators repeatedly confront inherent cognitive limitations, including vigilance decrements, signal – noise imbalance, and difficulties in maintaining coherent mental models under alarm floods." (p. 1)
